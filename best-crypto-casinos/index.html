Why crypto is the fastest way to be paid — and what it costs

Across our 214 timed withdrawals, cryptocurrency had a median of 3 hours 10 minutes against 11 hours 45 minutes on e-wallets and 2.4 business days on cards. Spino, which settles only in crypto, managed a median of 22 minutes.

The reason is structural. An e-wallet withdrawal is a payment instruction that a human eventually approves, batched by a payments team working European hours. A crypto withdrawal is a transaction broadcast to a network that does not know what time it is in Malta. Once approved, it settles.

What it costs you is the network fee, the exchange spread on getting in and out of crypto in the first place, and — if you use a volatile coin — price movement while your balance sits there.

The practical recommendation

Use a dollar-pegged stablecoin on a cheap network. USDT on Tron or USDC on Solana gives you crypto’s settlement speed without crypto’s price risk, at a fee of cents rather than dollars. A NZ$500 balance in USDT is NZ$500 tomorrow. A NZ$500 balance in Bitcoin might be NZ$430.

The trap to avoid: sending the right coin on the wrong network. USDT exists on Ethereum, Tron, Solana, BNB Chain and others, and the fee difference between the cheapest and dearest is more than twentyfold. Worse, sending to an address on the wrong network usually loses the funds permanently. Read the cashier screen, match the network exactly, and send a small test amount first if the sum is large.

Tax: the part every other page gets wrong

“Gambling winnings are tax-free in New Zealand” is true and, for a crypto player, dangerously incomplete.

We are a review site, not your accountant, and this is general information rather than tax advice. If the sums are material, talk to a chartered accountant or read Inland Revenue’s own guidance on cryptoassets.

Two separate questions, routinely collapsed into one.

Question one: is the casino win taxable?

For a casual player, no. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational gambling winnings as assessable income. Nothing to declare.

Question two: is the crypto gain taxable?

Potentially yes, and this is the bit that catches people. IRD treats cryptoassets as property. Property acquired for the purpose of disposal is taxable on disposal — and buying crypto specifically to fund a casino account, then converting back to New Zealand dollars, is a fairly clear acquisition for the purpose of disposal.

So the gambling win is not taxed, but the movement in the crypto’s New Zealand dollar value between buying it and converting back can be.

A worked example

  1. You buy 2,000 USDT for NZ$3,300.
  2. You deposit it, play, and finish with 2,600 USDT. The 600 USDT you won is not taxable income.
  3. You withdraw 2,600 USDT and convert it back, receiving NZ$4,420 — the NZD/USD rate having moved in your favour.
  4. Your cost base for the 2,000 USDT you bought was NZ$3,300. The equivalent NZD value of that original 2,000 USDT at the exit rate is NZ$3,400.
  5. The NZ$100 difference is a gain on property, not a gambling win, and it is potentially taxable.
  6. The 600 USDT you won — roughly NZ$1,020 at the exit rate — is a gambling win and is not.

Keep records: the date and NZD value of every purchase, every deposit, every withdrawal and every conversion. If you use a stablecoin the gains are small and the record-keeping is simple, which is another argument for USDT over Bitcoin. Full detail on tax on gambling winnings in NZ.

Frequently asked questions

Are crypto casinos legal in New Zealand?

Using one is not an offence for you, exactly as with any other offshore online casino. The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 regulates the supply of online casino gambling to New Zealanders and requires a DIA licence from 1 December 2026; the obligation is on the operator. Cryptocurrency itself is entirely legal to hold and trade in New Zealand, and is treated by IRD as property.

Which cryptocurrency is best for online casinos?

A dollar-pegged stablecoin on a low-fee network: USDT on Tron or USDC on Solana. You get settlement in minutes for a fee of cents, with no exposure to price movement while your balance sits in the account. Bitcoin is the slowest and among the dearest, and its price volatility means a NZ$500 balance might not be NZ$500 tomorrow.

Do I pay tax on crypto casino winnings in NZ?

Not on the gambling win itself — casual winnings are not assessable income. But IRD treats cryptoassets as property, and a gain on crypto acquired for the purpose of disposal can be taxable. So the win is not taxed and the currency movement may be. We work a full example above and on the tax page.

Is a crypto casino safe?

The chain is not the issue — the operator is. A crypto casino is exactly as trustworthy as its licence, its named operating company and its payment record. What crypto does change is reversibility: a card chargeback exists, a blockchain transaction does not. That makes the choice of operator more consequential, not less. Spino, our top crypto pick, holds a Tobique licence, which is the least established regulator in our set — we say so on its review.

What is a provably fair game?

A mechanism, common on crypto-native sites, that lets you verify a result was not manipulated. The operator publishes a hashed server seed before the round; you supply a client seed; after the round both are revealed and you can recompute the outcome. It is genuinely useful for in-house crash and dice games. It does not apply to third-party pokies from Pragmatic Play or NetEnt, which are certified by test labs instead.

Usually not. Almost every operator requires withdrawals to the source of deposit for at least the deposited amount, under anti-money-laundering rules. Deposit in crypto and you will be withdrawing in crypto, then converting at your exchange. Plan the exit before you make the entry.
