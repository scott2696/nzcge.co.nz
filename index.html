New Zealand’s online casino law is changing right now

This is the single biggest gap in the pages currently outranking us. Most still describe New Zealand as an unregulated offshore market. It has not been one since 1 May 2026.

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 came into force on 1 May 2026 and created New Zealand’s first domestic licensing regime for online casino gambling, administered by the Department of Internal Affairs. It is deliberately tight: fifteen licences, awarded through an ascending-clock auction, no more than three to any one applicant, one brand per licence.

The 1 December cutoff

What this means for you, plainly. No New Zealand law makes it an offence for an individual to gamble at an online casino, and the Act does not change that. The obligation it creates falls on the operator. But from 1 December 2026 an operator that has not applied for a licence must stop serving New Zealanders — so the practical risk is not legal, it is continuity. Do not carry a large balance into December. Withdraw as you go.

We track the licensing programme week by week, with every date sourced to the DIA or the Act itself, on is online gambling legal in NZ. It is the page we would read first if we were opening an account this month.

One more New Zealand-specific point that international comparison sites routinely get wrong: the legal gambling age here is 18 for online gambling, lotteries and TAB betting, and 20 for a land-based casino floor. Two numbers, not one.

Is online gambling banned in New Zealand?

No. Online gambling is not banned in New Zealand, and it is not an offence for you to play. Two laws changed recently and both were widely reported as bans. Neither creates an offence for the player. The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 regulates who may supply online casino gambling to New Zealanders; the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 does the same for sports and racing betting. In both cases the obligation sits on the operator.

We lead with this because our search data says it is the single biggest misunderstanding in the market. Thirty-two of the 994 queries we harvested ask about legality, and the ones using the word “banned” are among the most common of all.

Here is the distinction that everything turns on.

So why does everyone think it is banned?

Because “New Zealand bans offshore betting” is a true headline about the supply side and a false impression about the demand side, and headlines do not carry that distinction. The Racing Industry Amendment Act genuinely did end offshore sportsbooks’ ability to lawfully operate here. It also expressly protected the punter. Only one half of that made the news.

What you actually give up

Not your liberty. Your recourse. No New Zealand regulator stands behind an offshore operator, the DIA cannot act on your complaint about one, and New Zealand’s self-exclusion framework does not reach them. That is a real cost and it should shape how you use an offshore account — keep the balance small, verify on day one, withdraw rather than accumulate — rather than whether you do.

The four clauses that cost players the most

1. Game weighting

Pokies usually count 100% toward wagering. Live blackjack and roulette typically count 10–20%, and some titles count zero. A NZ$8,000 requirement cleared on live blackjack at 10% weighting is NZ$80,000 of table play. If you intend to clear a bonus, clear it on pokies.

2. Maximum bet while wagering

Almost universally NZ$5 to NZ$10 per spin. Exceed it once, even accidentally, and the operator is entitled to void the bonus and everything won with it. This is the most common single cause of a forfeited balance, and it usually happens because someone bought a bonus feature at a stake above the cap.

Several offers cap what you can withdraw from bonus funds — commonly 5x or 10x the bonus, regardless of what you win. Turn NZ$50 of free spins into NZ$4,000 under a 10x cap and you withdraw NZ$500.

4. Expiry

Typically 7 to 30 days. A 40x requirement inside seven days is not an offer, it is a decoration.
