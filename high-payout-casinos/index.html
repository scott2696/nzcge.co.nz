What “effective RTP” means and how we get it

Casinos do not publish a site-wide RTP figure, and if they did it would be unverifiable. So we build one. We take a fixed basket of identical headline titles — Gates of Olympus, Sweet Bonanza, Book of Dead, Big Bass Bonanza, Starburst, Razor Shark, Money Train 4 and around thirty others depending on availability — open each one in the lobby from a New Zealand IP, and read the RTP from the in-game information panel.

That figure is the one that binds. Averaged across the basket it gives a directly comparable number: what this lobby actually returns on the games most people play.

The high-build deployment column is the more damning one. It is the percentage of sampled titles where the lobby runs at or near the studio’s published maximum RTP. At Smash it is 92%. At Slotsgem it is 61% — meaning on nearly four titles in ten you are playing a deliberately reduced version.

Why this is not on any competitor page

Because it takes about six hours per casino and produces an answer some operators would rather not have published. Every ranking page we examined across five markets either ignores RTP entirely or quotes the studio’s theoretical maximum as though every lobby ran it. Neither is useful.

RTP meaning at a casino, and what is RTP in pokies?

RTP meaning, in one sentence: return to player is the percentage of everything staked on a game that the game pays back over the long run. A pokie at 96% RTP returns NZ$96 for every NZ$100 wagered, averaged across millions of spins. The 4% it keeps is the house edge, and that is the real price of playing.

What is RTP in pokies specifically?

Every pokie has a published RTP set by the studio and certified by an independent test lab. You can read it yourself: open the game, tap the “i” or paytable icon, and scroll to the bottom. The figure shown there is the one that binds — not the studio’s marketing number, and not whatever a comparison page claims.

What is a good RTP percentage?

  • 97%+ — excellent, and uncommon outside a handful of titles.
  • 96–97% — good. This is the band the highest RTP casinos NZ players can reach actually operate in.
  • 95–96% — average.
  • Below 95% — worth avoiding, because the same game is usually available elsewhere at a higher configuration.

How is casino RTP calculated?

Total returned to players divided by total staked, across a very large number of simulated and live game rounds, verified by a test lab against the game’s mathematical model. It is not a promise about your session; it is a property of the model.

House edge vs RTP

They are the same number from opposite ends: house edge = 100% − RTP. A 96% RTP pokie has a 4% house edge. RTP is the convention for pokies, house edge for table games, and that is the only real difference. Blackjack is usually quoted as a 0.43% house edge rather than a 99.57% RTP, but the arithmetic is identical — and the comparison is the useful bit: blackjack RTP vs pokies RTP is roughly 99.6% against 96%, which is why table games are weighted at 10–20% toward bonus wagering and pokies at 100%.

Does RTP matter in the short term?

Barely. Over an evening, variance dominates completely — a 96% pokie will take your whole balance regularly and a 94% one will occasionally pay for a holiday. RTP decides what happens across thousands of sessions, not one. What it does tell you, reliably, is which lobby is quietly charging you more for the same game.

The practical consequence is larger than most players realise. Suppose you play for two hours a week at roughly NZ$300 of turnover per hour — unremarkable for a pokies session at NZ$1 a spin.

  • On blackjack with basic strategy: NZ$31,200 turnover a year × 0.43% = NZ$134 a year.
  • On a high-RTP pokie: × 3.5% = NZ$1,092 a year.
  • On a low-RTP build of the same pokie: × 5.75% = NZ$1,794 a year.
  • On American roulette: × 5.26% = NZ$1,641 a year.

Same hours, same stakes, a thirteen-fold difference in cost. None of this makes any of it a bad way to spend an evening — it makes it a priced one, and the price is knowable in advance.

Three rules that follow directly

  1. Never play American roulette when European is on the same screen. Identical game, double the cost. This is the easiest money you will ever save.
  2. Check the video poker paytable before you play. A 9/6 Jacks or Better machine is 0.46%; an 8/5 version of what looks like the same game is 2.7%. The numbers refer to the full-house and flush payouts and they are on the paytable.
  3. Check the pokie’s RTP in the info panel. Ten seconds, potentially two percentage points.

The things a high payout percentage does not tell you

RTP is the most useful single number in this market and it is routinely over-read. Four things it does not mean.

It says nothing about your session

96% RTP is measured over millions of spins. Over an evening, variance dominates completely. A 96% pokie will take your whole balance often, and a 94% pokie will occasionally pay for your holiday. RTP tells you what happens if you play forever, which you will not.

It does not survive a wagering requirement

A 40x bonus requirement on a NZ$200 bonus means NZ$8,000 of turnover. At 96% RTP the expected cost of that turnover is NZ$320. The RTP is excellent and the transaction is still negative. This is why we convert every bonus into dollars on the bonus page.

It is a different number on a progressive

A progressive jackpot pokie advertising 96% might return 88% in the base game, with the remaining 8% flowing to a jackpot pool that almost no one wins. The advertised figure is honest and the experience is quite different from a 96% fixed-jackpot title.

It does not account for the withdrawal cap

The highest-RTP lobby in the world is not much use if the weekly withdrawal ceiling means a large win arrives over two months. Read this page alongside fast payout casinos, where the caps are published.

What does RTP actually mean?

Return to player: the percentage of total stakes a game pays back over the long run. A 96% pokie returns NZ$96 per NZ$100 staked, averaged over millions of spins. The complement — 4% — is the house edge, and that is the real cost of playing.

Is a 94% RTP pokie bad?

It is worse than the same game at 96.5%, which is usually available elsewhere, and that is the point. On NZ$10,000 of annual turnover the difference is NZ$250. Since the high-RTP build of most headline titles exists and some lobbies deploy it, there is rarely a reason to accept the low one.

Do live dealer games have better RTP than pokies?

Generally yes. Live blackjack with correct basic strategy runs about 99.5%, live baccarat on the banker bet about 98.9%, and European roulette 97.3% — all better than a typical pokie at 96%. The catch is bonus weighting: table games usually count only 10–20% toward wagering requirements, precisely because their edge is so low.

Can a casino change a game's RTP?

Not mid-session, and not arbitrarily. But it can choose which certified configuration of a title to license and deploy, and studios supply several. That choice is made at the lobby level, is disclosed in the in-game information panel, and is the reason two casinos with identical game lists can pay back a percentage point apart.
