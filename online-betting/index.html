What the law actually says

On 28 June 2025 the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 came into force, having received Royal Assent on 27 June. It extended TAB NZ’s long-standing monopoly on racing and sports betting from land-based wagering to cover online betting as well.

The purpose was economic. TAB NZ estimated that New Zealanders were betting NZ$180 to NZ$200 million a year with offshore operators, money that generated no betting duty, no point-of-consumption charges and no funding for the New Zealand racing industry or community sport. A large share of it was on NBA basketball, a market TAB NZ has never priced as competitively as the global books.

Where the Act places the obligation

This is the part that matters and the part most coverage flattens. It is now unlawful for any person other than TAB NZ or its partner organisation to offer or promote racing betting, sports betting, or other racing or sports betting to a person in New Zealand.

That binds operators and promoters. It does not bind you. The legislation expressly provides that an individual may not be convicted of an offence for placing a bet with an offshore betting operator.

We state this as plainly as we can because a great deal of published commentary has compressed it into “offshore betting is now illegal in New Zealand”. That sentence is accurate about supply and actively misleading about you.

What Parliament deliberately did not do

It did not introduce ISP-level blocking of offshore betting sites. The policy work considered it and rejected it: URLs are fluid, the number of operators is large, and a blocklist is out of date the day it is published. The Act relies on operator liability instead — holding offshore bookmakers responsible for taking bets from people inside New Zealand.

Whether that is enforceable in practice against a company incorporated in Curaçao is a fair question, and the honest answer nearly two years on is that it is largely untested.

What you actually lose

Not your liberty. Your recourse.

  • No New Zealand regulator. The Department of Internal Affairs does not supervise an offshore book and cannot act on your complaint about one.
  • No domestic dispute process. TAB NZ has one; an offshore book has whatever its own licensing authority provides, which in Curaçao is a real but slow process and in some jurisdictions is close to nothing.
  • No harm-minimisation framework. New Zealand’s self-exclusion and limit-setting regime does not reach offshore operators. Their tools are voluntary.
  • No guarantee of continuity. An operator may simply stop serving New Zealand, with your balance in the account.

Those are real costs and they should shape how you use an offshore account rather than whether you do: keep the balance small, verify on day one, and withdraw winnings rather than letting them accumulate.

Racing: why this one is not a close call

For New Zealand thoroughbred and harness racing, TAB NZ is both the lawful option and, on the merits, clearly the better product. Four reasons.

The pools are domestic

New Zealand racing is bet into totalisator pools operated here. The dividend reflects New Zealand money and is calculated on New Zealand turnover. An offshore book pricing a Te Rapa maiden is quoting a fixed price off someone else’s market, generally with a wider margin and materially lower limits.

The exotics only exist at the tote

Trifectas, First 4, quinellas, Pick 6 and the multi-leg pools are a large share of serious racing play in New Zealand and offshore books do not offer them. If exotics are how you bet, there is no offshore alternative at all.

The data is complete

Full New Zealand form, sectional times, track conditions and real-time scratchings. Offshore books typically carry a stripped feed and update scratchings late, which on a small field is the difference between a bet and a refund.

Where the money goes

Betting duty and point-of-consumption charges on racing turnover fund the New Zealand racing industry directly — the clubs, the stakes, the infrastructure. That was the explicit purpose of the 2025 amendment.

The international exception

For the Melbourne Cup carnival, Royal Ascot, the Breeders’ Cup and the Hong Kong internationals, both routes work and TAB NZ co-mingles into the overseas pools for the major meetings, which usually beats an offshore fixed price. For a midweek Wolverhampton card, an offshore book may be the only place you can get a bet on at all.

Reading a price properly

Three things worth knowing that turn a headline price into information.

Decimal odds are an implied probability

Divide 1 by the decimal price. A price of 2.50 implies 1 ÷ 2.50 = 40%. A price of 1.40 implies 71.4%. Once every selection in a market is expressed this way you can add them up, and the total is the overround.

The overround tells you the charge

A two-way market priced 1.90 / 1.90 sums to 52.6% + 52.6% = 105.3%. The 5.3 points above 100 is the book’s margin. The same market at 1.95 / 1.95 sums to 102.6% — half the charge, for a price difference that looks trivial on the screen.

Best-price shopping is the highest-value habit in betting

Holding two or three accounts and taking the best available price on each bet is worth more over a season than any welcome offer, any tipping service and any system. On a NZ$10,000 annual turnover, consistently taking a price 2% better is NZ$200 — and unlike a bonus, it recurs every year and has no wagering condition attached.

That is the practical argument for holding a TAB NZ account and an offshore account: not disloyalty to either, just arithmetic.

Betting responsibly

Betting carries a specific risk that casino play does not: it feels like a skill game, because it partly is. That makes it much easier to believe a losing run is bad luck rather than a margin doing exactly what it is designed to do.

Three habits that matter more than any tip:

  • Stake a fixed unit. One to two percent of a defined bankroll per bet. Not “more on the ones I like”, which is how a bad Saturday becomes a bad month.
  • Never chase. The impulse to recover a loss with a bigger bet on a worse market is the single most reliable way to turn a manageable loss into an unmanageable one.
  • Keep a record. Every bet, stake and result. Most people who believe they are roughly breaking even are not, and a spreadsheet is the cheapest way to find out.

Online betting in New Zealand — your questions answered

Is online betting legal in New Zealand?

For you, yes — unambiguously. The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025, in force since 28 June 2025, makes it unlawful for anyone other than TAB NZ and its partner to offer or promote racing and sports betting to a person in New Zealand. That prohibition binds the supplier. The legislation expressly provides that an individual may not be convicted of an offence for placing a bet with an offshore betting operator. If you bet from Wellington with a bookmaker in Curaçao, you have not committed an offence. A great deal of coverage has compressed this into “offshore betting is illegal in New Zealand”, which is accurate about supply and misleading about you.

What actually changed on 28 June 2025?

TAB NZ’s long-standing monopoly on racing and sports betting, which previously covered land-based wagering, was extended to cover online betting as well. The stated purpose was to protect the financial sustainability of the New Zealand racing industry, which was losing an estimated NZ$180–200 million a year in turnover to offshore operators. Parliament deliberately stopped short of geo-blocking offshore sites, on the basis that URLs are fluid and the number of operators makes blocking impractical. The chosen mechanism is operator liability instead.

Can I get in trouble for using an offshore bookmaker?

No. There is no offence for the punter, no penalty, and no enforcement mechanism aimed at individuals. What you give up is not legal safety — it is recourse. No New Zealand regulator stands behind an offshore book. If it withholds your money, the Department of Internal Affairs cannot help you and TAB NZ’s dispute process does not apply. Your only route is the book’s own licensing authority, wherever that is.

Should I use TAB NZ or an offshore bookmaker?

Both, for different things — which is what most experienced New Zealand punters actually do. TAB NZ for New Zealand racing, without qualification: the domestic totalisator pools, the exotics, full form and sectional data, and it is the lawful option. Offshore for international sport, where the pricing is measurably sharper: our sampling puts offshore 2.4 percentage points better on NBA match odds and 2.6 better on the Premier League. For NPC, Heartland and netball, TAB NZ is better priced as well as lawful.

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in New Zealand?

Not as a casual punter. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational betting winnings as assessable income, whether you bet with TAB NZ or offshore. The exception is if betting is genuinely your business — systematic, organised and undertaken for profit — in which case profits may be taxable and losses deductible. That is a high bar and very few people meet it. If you fund an offshore account with cryptocurrency there is a separate question about the crypto itself; see tax on gambling winnings.

Four reasons, and they are structural rather than promotional. Pools: New Zealand thoroughbred and harness racing is bet into totalisator pools operated here, so the dividends reflect New Zealand money. Exotics: trifectas, First 4, quinellas and multi-leg pools make up a large share of serious racing play and offshore books do not offer them. What is an overround and why does it matter more than the odds?

The overround is the book’s theoretical margin, calculated by converting every price in a market to an implied probability and adding them up. A market summing to 100% has no margin; one summing to 106% has a 6% margin built in. It matters more than any single price because it tells you what the book charges across the whole market rather than on the one selection you happened to look at. A 2-percentage-point difference in overround, applied to NZ$10,000 of annual turnover, is NZ$200 — considerably more than any welcome offer is worth.

Can I use both TAB NZ and an offshore account?

Yes. Nothing prevents it, there is no register linking them, and it is the sensible arrangement. TAB NZ for domestic racing and the New Zealand competitions it prices well; an offshore book for the NBA, European football, esports and in-play, where the pricing and depth are genuinely better. Keep the offshore balance small and withdraw winnings rather than letting them accumulate, because that is the account with no regulator behind it.

Are offshore bookmakers going to be blocked?

Not under the current legislation. Parliament considered ISP-level blocking and expressly declined, on the basis that URLs change faster than a blocklist can and the number of operators makes it impractical. The Act relies on making offshore operators liable for taking bets from within New Zealand instead. Whether that is enforceable against a company in Curaçao is a fair question and the honest answer is that it is largely untested.

What happens to my offshore betting account now?

Practically, nothing has changed for you since June 2025 — the books that accepted New Zealand registrations before mostly still do. The risk is not legal, it is continuity: an operator may decide the New Zealand market is not worth the exposure and withdraw, with your balance in it. The same advice applies as for online casinos. Verify your account on day one, keep the balance small, and withdraw winnings rather than letting them sit.
