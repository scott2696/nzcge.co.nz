The short answer

It is not an offence for you to gamble at an online casino in New Zealand. No New Zealand law makes it one, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 did not change that. What the Act regulates is the supply of online casino gambling to New Zealanders, which now requires a licence from the Department of Internal Affairs. From 1 December 2026 — 71 days away — an operator that has not applied for one must stop serving New Zealand players.

That distinction — between the player and the supplier — runs through all of New Zealand gambling law and it is the thing most international comparison sites get wrong. It is worth understanding properly, because it determines what your actual risk is, and it is not a legal one.

The licensing programme, stage by stage

Every date here is sourced to the Act or to the DIA’s published provider guidance. The status labels are computed at build time, so a stale page is visibly stale.

  1. 1 May 2026Complete

    Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 in force

    The Act commences, creating New Zealand's first domestic online casino licensing regime and replacing an unregulated offshore market.

  2. 17 July 2026Complete

    Expressions of interest open

    Stage one of three. NZ$19,000 + GST per expression of interest, non-refundable. Applicants must evidence NZ$7.5m minimum capital.

  3. 14 August 2026Complete

    Expressions of interest close

    Around fifty expressions of interest were lodged for fifteen licences. The DIA has confirmed the round was oversubscribed and will not name who applied.

  4. 29 September 2026Next

    Licence auction

    A multi-round simultaneous ascending clock auction. The price rises in steps until demand matches the fifteen licences available. Participants are not made public.

  5. 1 October 2026Upcoming

    Full applications open

    Successful bidders file business plans, advertising and marketing strategy, AML/CFT programme and harm-minimisation measures.

  6. 1 December 2026Upcoming

    Unlicensed operators must stop

    Any operator that has not applied must stop offering online casino gambling to New Zealanders. Operators with a pending application may continue to operate, but not advertise, until it is decided.

  7. 1 January 2027Upcoming

    Duty rises to 16%

    The offshore gambling duty on licensed online casino operators rises from 12% to 16% of gross gambling revenue, alongside 15% GST and the 1.24% problem gambling levy.

  8. 31 March 2027Estimated

    Licensed market expected live

    Government expects the regulated market to be operational in early 2027. This quarter is our estimate, not a published date.

  9. 1 June 2027Upcoming

    Transitional arrangement ends outright

    An operator that applied before 1 December 2026 may keep serving New Zealanders — without advertising — until its application is decided or 1 June 2027, whichever comes first. After that the transition is over and unlicensed supply is simply unlawful.

The design is deliberately restrictive. Fifteen licences for a market the government valued at well over half a billion dollars a year means most of the operators currently serving New Zealanders will not be here in 2027. Roughly fifty expressions of interest were lodged for those fifteen places.

The barriers are substantial and intentionally so: NZ$19,000 plus GST, non-refundable, simply to lodge an expression of interest; NZ$7.5 million of minimum capital; a three-year term renewable for five; a maximum of three licences to any one applicant, one brand each.

What licensees will be required to do

  • Verify age and identity before play, to a New Zealand standard
  • Offer deposit, loss and session limits, and self-exclusion
  • Operate an AML/CFT programme under New Zealand law
  • Pay 15% GST, the offshore gambling duty (12%, rising to 16% from 1 January 2027) and the 1.24% problem gambling levy
  • Comply with advertising restrictions — notably, affiliate marketing by licensees is prohibited

Are online casinos legal in New Zealand? The 2026 position

Online gambling laws New Zealand operates under changed twice in eighteen months, and the coverage of both changes has left most people with the wrong impression. Here is each question answered separately.

Is it legal for me to play?

Yes. No New Zealand statute makes it an offence for an individual to gamble at an online casino, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 did not create one. Every offence in the Act attaches to supply.

Are there licensed online casinos NZ players can use today?

No — not yet. This is the part most pages get wrong. There is currently no such thing as a DIA licensed online casino: not one licence has been issued. Every operator serving New Zealanders is licensed offshore, in Curaçao, Anjouan or Tobique. Any site claiming a New Zealand licence right now is misrepresenting itself, and that alone is reason enough to close the tab.

Regulated online casinos New Zealand players can use are expected from early 2027, once the DIA issues the first of its fifteen licences. There is no New Zealand online casino licence list to publish yet — the DIA has not named the applicants and will not until licences are granted. When it does, we will publish the list of licence holders here and on new casinos NZ, dated.

So what will legal online casinos NZ players can use look like from 2027?

From 2027 there will be a genuine two-tier market for the first time. A legal online casino NZ players can use will hold a DIA licence, be required to offer enforceable deposit limits and self-exclusion, run an AML programme under New Zealand law, and answer to a regulator that can act on the licence. Unlicensed online casinos NZ residents can still reach will have none of that — and supplying to New Zealanders will by then be unlawful for them.

Which online casinos are licensed in NZ?

None, today. That is the complete and accurate answer, and it is worth being blunt about because the question has real search volume and the honest response is not the one most pages give.

Offshore casinos NZ legal status, summarised

  • For you: not an offence, now or after the transition.
  • For them, until 1 December 2026: permitted under a transitional arrangement, without advertising.
  • From 1 December 2026: an operator that has not applied must stop serving New Zealanders.
  • From 1 June 2027: the transition ends outright even for applicants awaiting a decision.
  • Penalty for getting it wrong: up to NZ$5 million for a company, NZ$300,000 for an individual.

What happens to offshore casinos NZ players use after December 2026 is therefore a commercial question rather than a legal one for you: some will apply and stay, some will exit, and your exposure is whatever balance you left behind. What to do about it.

Sports and racing betting is a completely separate regime

And a stricter one. Conflating the two is the most common error on competitor pages.

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 governs online casino gambling. It has nothing to do with betting on sport or racing, which is governed by the Racing Industry Act 2020 as amended by the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025.

That amendment came into force on 28 June 2025 and extended TAB NZ’s monopoly on racing and sports betting to cover online betting. It is now unlawful for anyone other than TAB NZ and its partner to offer or promote racing or sports betting to a person in New Zealand.

The same player/supplier distinction applies, and here it is explicit in the legislation: an individual may not be convicted of an offence for placing a bet with an offshore betting operator.

Two regimes, two different sets of dates, one shared principle. Full treatment on online betting NZ.

The framework underneath: the Gambling Act 2003

The 2026 Act sits on top of the Gambling Act 2003, which remains the primary statute and still governs almost everything else about gambling in New Zealand.

The 2003 Act works by classifying gambling into four classes and prohibiting anything not expressly authorised. Under it, “remote interactive gambling” — broadly, online gambling — conducted from within New Zealand has long been prohibited unless authorised. Gambling conducted from overseas sat outside its reach, which is precisely the gap the 2026 Act was passed to close.

Critically, section 9(2)(b) and the enforcement architecture around it aim at the provider. The 2003 Act has never made it an offence for a New Zealander to participate in overseas online gambling, and the DIA has consistently said so in its own public guidance.

Ages, which are two numbers and not one

  • 18 — online gambling, Lotto, Instant Kiwi, TAB betting, and class 4 pokies in pubs and clubs
  • 20 — entry to a land-based casino floor (SkyCity, Christchurch Casino, Dunedin)

This is genuinely New Zealand-specific and international templates routinely state one age and get the other wrong.

Land-based pokies, for contrast

Around 14,000 class 4 machines operate in pubs and clubs, with a NZ$2.50 maximum stake and a NZ$500 maximum prize, and a legal minimum return of 78%. Online pokies at the sites we review have no stake cap, no prize cap and typically return 94–97%. Better odds, far higher exposure — a trade-off worth understanding before assuming online is simply the same thing on a screen.

What you should actually do about all this

  1. Do not carry a large balance into December

    The 1 December cutoff is 71 days away. An operator that has not applied must stop serving New Zealanders. Your risk is not legal, it is continuity — money in your bank account is unaffected by an operator's licensing problems; money in your casino balance is not.

  2. Complete verification now

    If an operator does wind down its New Zealand business, an unverified account is the hardest one to get money out of. Upload ID and proof of address today.

  3. Keep your own transaction records

    Dates and amounts of every deposit and withdrawal. If you ever need to prove a balance to an operator or a liquidator, your own records are the evidence.

  4. Prefer operators that have publicly signalled an application

    The DIA will not name applicants, but some operators have said publicly that they intend to seek a New Zealand licence. An operator planning to be here in 2027 has a strong reason to treat 2026 players well.

  5. Watch for the licensed market in early 2027

    When New Zealand-licensed casinos launch, they will bring enforceable harm-minimisation tools, a domestic complaints route and a regulator that can actually act. For most players that will be a straightforwardly better product than anything available today.

Frequently asked questions

Is it illegal to play at an online casino in New Zealand?

No. No New Zealand law makes it an offence for an individual to gamble at an online casino, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 did not introduce one. The Act regulates supply: offering online casino gambling to New Zealanders now requires a DIA licence, and unlicensed operators must stop from 1 December 2026. The obligation is on the operator.

What is the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026?

New Zealand’s first domestic online casino licensing regime, in force since 1 May 2026 and administered by the Department of Internal Affairs. It caps the market at fifteen licences awarded by ascending-clock auction, with a maximum of three per applicant and one brand per licence. Licensees must meet New Zealand harm-minimisation, AML and advertising rules, and pay GST, gambling duty and the problem gambling levy.

When do licensed online casinos launch in New Zealand?

Early 2027 on the government’s own expectation. The auction ran in late September 2026, full applications opened on 1 October, and licences are expected to be issued in the first quarter of 2027. Our first-quarter estimate is ours, not a published date — the DIA has not committed to a launch day.

What happens to offshore casinos after 1 December 2026?

An operator that has not applied for a licence must stop offering online casino gambling to New Zealanders. One that has applied may continue to operate while its application is decided, but may not advertise here. In practice some operators will exit the market and others will continue quietly. The player-side risk is continuity, not prosecution.

Can the DIA block offshore gambling websites?

Not under the current legislation, for either casino or betting. Parliament considered ISP-level blocking in the context of the 2025 betting amendment and expressly declined, on the basis that URLs are fluid and the number of operators makes a blocklist impractical. The chosen mechanism in both regimes is operator liability.

What is the legal gambling age in New Zealand?

Eighteen for online gambling, Lotto, Instant Kiwi, TAB betting and pub pokies. Twenty for entry to a land-based casino floor. Two different numbers, and international comparison sites regularly state one and get the other wrong.

Do offshore casinos pay tax in New Zealand?

Currently they pay the offshore gambling duty and GST on supplies to New Zealand consumers where those obligations apply. Under the new regime licensees will pay 15% GST, an offshore gambling duty rising from 12% to 16% on 1 January 2027, and a 1.24% problem gambling levy funding harm-prevention services.
