The four gates a casino passes before we score it

Scoring comes after filtering. A brand that fails any of these does not get a score, it gets left off — with the single exception noted below.

  1. A named regulator. A licence number we can check on a public register. Not a seal image, not a “licensed and regulated” line with nothing behind it.
  2. A working New Zealand payment route. At least one rail that a New Zealander can actually use, tested from a New Zealand bank account. A great casino you cannot deposit at is not a great casino.
  3. A withdrawal that arrived. We do not list an operator we have not been paid by. Every site here has paid us at least five times.
  4. Terms we would explain to a friend without wincing. Highly subjective, deliberately. A clause that lets the operator void winnings at its sole discretion with no stated cause fails this.

The exception. Roby Casino fails gate one. We rank it anyway, last, with the failure stated on every page it appears on. The reasoning: readers search for it, and a search that finds only pages which do not mention the disclosure gap is worse for them than finding ours.

What gets a casino removed

Three things, and we have used all three: a pattern of reader complaints about withheld withdrawals; a licence that lapses and is not replaced; or a terms change that introduces a clause failing gate four. Removal is not reversible on request — a brand comes back only after a full re-test.

What the 2026 licensing transition means for your account

None of the casinos on this page holds a New Zealand licence, because none has been issued yet. The Department of Internal Affairs is part-way through a three-stage process under the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026: expressions of interest closed on 14 August 2026 with roughly fifty lodged for fifteen licences, an ascending-clock auction ran from 29 September, full applications opened on 1 October, and the market is expected to go live in early 2027.

The date on your calendar is 1 December 2026, now 71 days away. From then, an operator that has not applied must stop offering online casino gambling to New Zealanders. One that has applied may keep operating while the application is decided, but may not advertise here.

Three things to do before December. Withdraw winnings rather than accumulating a balance. Complete verification now, so a rushed withdrawal later is not held up by document checks. And keep your own record of deposits and withdrawals — if an operator exits the market, your transaction history is the evidence you will need.

What is the difference between a Curaçao and an Anjouan licence?

Both are offshore licences and neither is a New Zealand licence. Curaçao restructured its regime in 2024 under the Gaming Control Board, which brought direct licensing, a public register and a defined complaints route — a genuine improvement on the old master/sub-licence arrangement. Anjouan, in the Comoros, is newer, lighter-touch and largely untested on contested player complaints. In practice, a Curaçao GCB licence with a named operating company is currently the stronger position. Both are weaker than the New Zealand licences the DIA will issue in 2027.

Which online casinos take New Zealand dollars?

Ten of the fifteen we rank: Spinjo, Kingdom, Rooster Bet, Fortune Play, Lucky Vibe, Lucky Circus, Lucky7even, Rivo, Smash and MadCasino. CrownSlots, Gunsbet, Ivibet and Hellspin hold euros; Spino holds stablecoins. On a euro site you pay a conversion spread of about 2.4% each way, measured, which over a year of ordinary play usually outweighs a larger welcome bonus.

Can I have accounts at more than one casino?

Yes, and most experienced players do. There is no rule against it and no New Zealand register that links them. The sensible pattern is two or three accounts chosen for different jobs — one deep library for everyday play, one fast payer for withdrawals, one with a genuinely clearable bonus — rather than one account at the site with the biggest banner. The one thing not to do is open six accounts to farm welcome bonuses you have no intention of clearing; you will lose money doing it.

What should I do if a casino refuses to pay me?

Four steps, in order. One: get the refusal in writing from live chat or email, including the specific term relied on. Two: escalate internally — most operators have a formal complaints address distinct from support. Three: take it to the regulator named on the site, which is why we weight published licensing so heavily; a Curaçao GCB complaint names the operating company and enters a real process. Four: tell us at contact. We log every reader complaint against the operator and it feeds the trust score directly. That is the mechanism by which a site can lose its place on this page.

Are new online casinos safe to use?

A 2024 launch is not automatically risky, but it carries less evidence. Kingdom, Smash and Rivo all launched in 2024 and have been faultless in our testing; Gunsbet has traded since 2016 and that record is itself worth something no new site can offer. Judge a new casino on what is checkable now — is the operating company named, is the licence current and verifiable on the regulator's register, are the terms coherent, does a withdrawal arrive — and then keep your balance small until it has proved itself over several cashouts.

Do online casinos have a house edge on every game?

Yes, on every game, always. The size varies enormously and it is the most useful thing you can know. Blackjack with correct basic strategy runs about 0.5%. European roulette is 2.7%. American roulette is 5.26% — if a lobby offers both, there is no reason ever to play the American wheel. Pokies run roughly 3–8% depending on the title and the RTP configuration the operator deployed. Over a long enough period the edge is the only thing that decides the outcome.
